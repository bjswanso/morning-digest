Section 2 — Personal Care

**1. 🧠 Mental Health — Do a "cognitive offload" in 10 minutes.**

Your brain is a brilliant CPU running too many background processes. Before diving into work, spend 10 minutes writing every open loop rattling around in your head — tasks, worries, half-formed ideas — onto paper (or a notes app). Research consistently shows this "externalization" reduces cognitive load, improves working memory, and lowers low-grade anxiety. No editing, no organizing; just dump and close the tab.

**2. 🤝 Relationship with Spouse — Trade one genuine observation, not a question.**

Busy couples default to logistics ("Did you call the plumber?"). Tonight, instead of asking how their day was, *tell* your spouse one specific thing you noticed or appreciated about them this week — something behavioral, not generic. ("I noticed you handled that phone call so calmly.") Specificity signals attention, and attention is the currency of connection. Takes under two minutes; pays compound interest.

**3. 🔬 Science — Spend 10 minutes with the August Perseids preview.**

The Perseids this August come with an extraordinary bonus: the meteor shower peaks under a New Moon, just hours after the Moon crosses the Sun in a total solar eclipse — making August 2026 a blockbuster month for skywatching, featuring a total solar eclipse, a near-total lunar eclipse, and the Northern Hemisphere's biggest summer meteor shower. Pull up NASA's Perseid viewing guide, note your local peak time (night of Aug 11–12), and identify your nearest dark-sky spot. Planning it now means you'll actually go — and staring at a sky full of debris older than Earth is, empirically, a reliable cure for tunnel vision.
